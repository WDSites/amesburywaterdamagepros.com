How water gets into a crawl space here

Amesbury sits in the Boston area climate zone. The National Weather Service Boston normals for 1991 to 2020 put annual precipitation at 43.59 inches and show 91.6 days a year with a low at or below 32 degrees Fahrenheit. That is a lot of rain, melt and thaw cycles hitting a low, open space.

Older Amesbury housing is common, and the state's housing plan counts 43 percent of units as built before 1960. Older foundations often have stone or block walls with earthen or thin concrete floors. Water finds them in several ways.

  • Groundwater rising after a wet spell, snowmelt or a long rain.
  • Surface water running toward the foundation from a low yard, a downspout or a clogged gutter.
  • A leaking supply line, drain or water heater overflow running down through the floor.
  • A failed sump pump in a pit, if the crawl space has one.
  • A dryer vent or condensate line that discharges into the space.

The city's Community Resilience Building matrix names drainage and flood concerns around the Powow River, Back River and several undersized culverts. Even without a flood, poor drainage near a house can push water into low spaces.

What you notice from upstairs

You often smell it before you see it. Watch for these.

  • A damp, earthy smell that rises through floor registers or closets.
  • Floors that feel cold, spongy or bouncy over one area.
  • Cupped or buckled hardwood and gaps between boards.
  • Doors that stick or swell in the humid months.
  • Pests, rust on ducts or fasteners, and visible mold on the joists.

None of these prove standing water. A damp crawl space can also be a humidity problem with no puddle at all, and the fix is different.

Safety steps for the first hour

Stay out when it is wet.

A crawl space is a confined space with low clearance, poor air and possible hazards. Do not crawl in while it holds water or smells of fuel.

Think electrical first.

Wiring, junction boxes, a water heater feed or a sump pump may sit low. Do not touch anything in wet areas. Call an electrician or 911 about any hazard.

Stop the source.

If a pipe is leaking, close the shutoff and call a plumber. If it is rain or groundwater, report it to your insurer and photograph it from the access hatch.

Check heating equipment.

If a furnace, boiler or duct runs under the floor, leave it off until it has been checked.

Keep the hatch clear.

Move stored items away from the access point so a crew can reach the space.

How we dry a crawl space

A crawl space makes drying harder than a room. Air does not move, the soil keeps releasing moisture, and every surface is rough wood or masonry.

  1. Pump the standing water.Small submersible pumps and extraction units remove water from the low spots. Large volumes are drawn down in stages so walls are not stressed.
  2. Pull wet materials.Soaked insulation, wet vapor barrier sections and ruined stored items are bagged and removed.
  3. Read the framing.Moisture meters and thermal imaging show how wet joists, subfloor and sill plates are, and whether water has reached the rooms above.
  4. Dry with controlled air.Air movers and LGR or desiccant dehumidifiers run until readings reach a dry standard. Cold weather slows drying, so desiccant units are often a better match.
  5. Clean and treat.Surfaces are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial. HEPA air filtration is used if mold or dust is disturbed.

This follows published guidance, such as the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. The wet framing is also where a water mitigation plan keeps the damage from spreading upward into the house.

What we do not do

We do restoration. We do not install vapor barriers or encapsulation, grade soil, repair foundations or fix drainage. Those are other trades, and so is plumbing. If a drain line or sump failed, a plumber or drainage contractor needs to fix the cause. Otherwise the space will refill, and drying only buys you time.

Mold and the air in the house

Mold needs moisture and an organic surface, and a damp crawl space has both. The Massachusetts homeowner guide on mold says to dry wet areas within 24 hours and to discard porous materials that cannot be dried quickly. The state Housing Code, 105 CMR 410, expects leaks and floods to be dried inside 48 hours.

Air moves up from a crawl space into living areas through gaps around pipes and ducts. That is why a musty crawl space can make the house smell. If you already see growth, our mold remediation page covers containment and removal.

Insurance fit

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says standard homeowners policies do not cover flood, and that sump pump failure or water backup needs an added endorsement. A sudden plumbing break is usually treated differently from groundwater seepage. Check your policy and ask your insurer, and photograph the space through the hatch before anything is removed.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not close the vents and hope it dries. Do not lay new insulation over damp wood. Do not use a household fan to push damp air into the house. Do not enter alone. If your house is in a nearby community, see our Newburyport and Haverhill pages.

Crawl space questions

Is emergency crawl space water damage service available in Amesbury?

Yes. Call at any hour and dispatch will talk through what to do. Crews respond as availability and conditions allow, and no arrival time is promised. Do not enter a flooded crawl space, and keep clear of wet wiring.

Are crawl spaces common in Amesbury?

Less than basements. They appear under older homes, capes, additions and some bump-outs. If your home has a full basement, the basement page is the better fit.

Can you encapsulate or waterproof my crawl space?

No. We pump, dry and clean. Encapsulation, vapor barrier installation and waterproofing are separate trades, and they should happen after the space is dry.

Is a damp crawl space dangerous to enter?

It can be. Low clearance, poor air, electrical gear and mold are all possible. Look from the hatch and call a professional rather than crawling in.

Will mold grow if the crawl space stays wet?

It can. State guidance says to dry wet areas within 24 hours and discard porous materials that cannot dry quickly. Ask for moisture readings before new insulation goes back.
