A water loss moves through stages, and each stage has its own crew, equipment and scope. This page explains how the six services fit together so you can describe your problem clearly when you call (978) 252-8637.

Start with the stage you are in

If water is on the floor right now, emergency water removal is the service that pumps and extracts it. Pipe bursts, failed heaters and appliance leaks usually begin here, and the job hands off to drying as soon as the standing water is gone.

If the water is stopped but the building is still wet, water mitigation takes over. Crews map moisture, remove materials that cannot dry and run air movers and dehumidifiers until readings reach a dry standard. The Massachusetts homeowner guide on mold says to dry wet areas within 24 hours, and the state Housing Code requires leaks and floods to be dried within 48 hours.

When the water came from outside, such as a river, a storm or the sewer, it is usually Category 3 water and the job is flood damage restoration. Cleanup and disinfection are larger there, and the page covers basement flooding and sewage backup in detail.

After the water is gone

Water damage cleanup removes wet debris, sanitizes surfaces and clears odor. It often overlaps with mitigation, but it answers a different question: what has to leave the building and how the space is left clean.

Once everything is dry and clean, water damage repair rebuilds drywall, insulation, flooring, trim and paint. Repair is the planned stage, and it should only start when moisture readings confirm the structure is dry.

When mold is part of the problem

Mold needs moisture, and a wet wall that was not dried quickly can grow it. Mold remediation covers containment, removal and drying, plus testing and clearance questions that come up in home sales. It is a secondary service in our lineup, but it is a common reason for calls, so the page goes into depth.

What to expect on the phone

Dispatch will ask what is wet, where the water came from, how long the area has been wet and whether anyone has been near electrical equipment. Answers to those four questions decide which service starts first. Have your address and the nearest main road ready, and keep photos handy for your insurer. The line to call is (978) 252-8637.

Insurance coverage depends on the cause. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, gradual leaks often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer.
