Kitchen Water Damage Restoration in Amesbury, MA

A wet kitchen hides water under cabinets, behind toe kicks and in the subfloor, so this page walks through what gets pulled, in what order, and what can stay.

Call (978) 252-8637

Kitchens take on water often because so many lines meet in one place. The sink has two supply lines and a drain. The dishwasher has a supply and a discharge hose. Every connection sits inside a cabinet or behind an appliance.

This page is about the room and how water behaves inside it. The appliance leak page covers hoses and machines. Here the question is what the water has soaked and how to get the kitchen dry and rebuilt.

Why a kitchen holds water

Floors are often tile or vinyl over underlayment over subfloor. Cabinets sit on top with a closed toe kick along the front. Countertops are sealed at the wall and the sink, and those seams are where water first gets in.

Water that gets past the surface does not drain out. It spreads under the cabinets and into the subfloor and wall base behind them.

Amesbury's housing adds a wrinkle. Amesbury's housing skews old, with 35 percent of units pre-1940 according to the state housing plan. Older kitchens often have several layers of flooring and original plumbing, which makes water harder to trace. The city Water Department supplies the pressure behind those lines, drawing mainly on the Powow River according to its 2024 water quality report.

Signs the damage is bigger than the puddle

  • A toe kick that is soft, stained or pulling away from the cabinet.
  • Cabinet doors that no longer close flush or a drawer front that has swollen.
  • Particleboard sides that look puffy or crumble when pressed.
  • Countertop edges that lift near the sink or dishwasher.
  • A musty smell when you open the sink cabinet.
  • A ceiling stain or sagging drywall in the room below.

Any one of these is worth a call. Together they usually mean water has been moving under the cabinets for a while.

Safety steps in the first hour

Shut off the water.

Close the angle stop under the sink or the valve behind the dishwasher. If it will not turn, use the main shut-off for the house.

Kill power to the area.

Kitchen outlets sit close to wet floors. If water is near them or the panel, leave the room and have a licensed electrician check it before power comes back.

Stop using the dishwasher.

Running it pushes more water into a damaged space.

Empty the cabinet.

Move pots and cleaners out so the crew can see the base.

Photograph everything.

Cabinets, the floor, the appliance, the stain below and any failed part.

Call the right trades.

A plumber fixes the leak. We handle the drying and repair, and we do not do plumbing or electrical work.

Where we pull first

A kitchen cannot be dried like an empty room. Cabinets are fixed to the wall and the countertop sits on top. Removing everything would turn a small loss into a remodel, so the order matters.

  1. Meter and image.The crew reads moisture in the floor, cabinet boxes and wall, and scans with a thermal camera to find wet zones behind surfaces.
  2. Pull the cheap layers.Toe kicks come off first, then baseboard, to open the cavity under the cabinets for air.
  3. Remove what cannot dry.Soaked particleboard, wet insulation, and underlayment under vinyl or laminate are cut out. Solid wood doors and frames are usually saved.
  4. Extract and set equipment.Water is extracted, then air movers and LGR dehumidifiers go in. Drying holes or ports may be placed so air can move under the cabinets.
  5. Monitor.Readings are taken each day until the materials match dry reference points, and equipment then comes out.
  6. Rebuild.Subfloor repair, flooring, cabinet work and paint follow once everything is dry.

Dispatch is open 24/7, and response depends on how many crews are working, so no arrival time is named. The final stage is covered on our water damage repair page.

Cabinets that cannot be dried in place

Particleboard and MDF boxes swell when they soak and do not return to shape. Once the edges puff, the joints loosen and the box usually has to be replaced. Plywood and solid wood hold up better.

Fixed cabinets pose a particular problem. If the water went into the base of a run of cabinets that cannot be lifted, the crew may have to remove the countertop and the damaged boxes to dry the subfloor underneath. That is a bigger step, and it should be explained to you before it happens, with photos of what was found.

If the boxes are intact, the open toe kick is often enough access for air movers to dry the base.

Ceilings below the kitchen

Kitchen water often shows up first as a ceiling stain in the room below. By then the subfloor and the joist cavity are wet. The ceiling may need to be opened to dry the joists, and a sagging ceiling can fall, so stay out from under it.

Insurance and permits

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance explains that a burst pipe is usually a homeowners claim when it is sudden and accidental, while flood and slow seepage are treated differently. Check your policy and ask your insurer how it handles a kitchen leak that went unnoticed.

Keep the failed part and photograph the cabinet bases before anything is removed.

For permits, Amesbury's Inspectional Services files them online and issues electrical, plumbing and gas permits only to licensed Massachusetts tradespeople. The city page does not say whether drywall or flooring repair after water damage needs a permit, so ask Inspectional Services before a rebuild.

Common mistakes in a wet kitchen

  • Wiping the cabinets and pushing them back while the base stays wet.
  • Caulking the sink seam without finding the source.
  • Running a fan for a day and calling it dry.
  • Replacing the floor before the subfloor has been metered.

Kitchen water damage questions

Is emergency kitchen water damage service available in Amesbury?

Yes. A sink or dishwasher leak at midnight reaches the same 24/7 line as any other call, with no promised arrival. Close the shutoff under the sink, keep power off wet outlets and stay clear of a sagging ceiling below.

Do I have to tear out my cabinets after a leak?

Not always. Plywood and solid wood often dry in place once the toe kick is off. Particleboard that has swollen usually has to be replaced. A crew can tell you after metering the boxes.

How long does the kitchen need to dry?

It depends on the materials and how long they were wet. Daily readings guide it, and equipment stays until the structure reaches dry reference levels. The Massachusetts homeowner guide says to dry wet areas within 24 hours.

Can the dishwasher stay in place during drying?

Often it has to be pulled so the floor and base cabinets behind it can be reached. A plumber disconnects and reconnects it, since we do not do plumbing work.

Will the water in my kitchen be clean?

Supply line water starts clean, but it picks up dirt as it soaks materials. A dishwasher drain or a sink backup is worse. The crew sorts the water by category before deciding what to remove.
