Newburyport neighborhoods and how water reaches them

Newburyport is a city on the south bank of the Merrimack River near its mouth, settled in 1635 and a city since 1851. Water reaches its buildings from three directions: the river and tidal waterfront, Plum Island across the marsh, and aging plumbing in the dense downtown streets around Market Square. A water damage call in Newburyport almost always starts with one of those three.

The Market Square Historic District was added to the National Register in 1971, and many of the brick and wood-frame buildings around it hold shops below and apartments above. A leak on an upper floor can run through several units and into a shop basement, so the first call often involves a landlord, a tenant and a business owner at once. Dispatch can coordinate the scope with all of them.

On Plum Island, the Plum Island Turnpike drawbridge is the only road access, and local news reported storm flooding there in 2022 and waterfront flooding in 2024. If a storm is bringing surge, a crew's access to the island can depend on the bridge and the road, so tell dispatch your street address and whether the road is passable.

When water is spreading in Newburyport

A burst pipe in an old Market Square building, a failed sump on a street near the river or a flooded Plum Island ground floor each count as an emergency. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, so call at any hour and describe what you see. Same-day response is available when crews are available, and we do not promise an arrival time we cannot keep.

Water near an outlet, a panel or a gas appliance is a safety issue before it is a property issue. Stay out of the space and call the utility or 911 first. Sewage or tidal water is Category 3 water under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which means porous materials usually come out and the space is disinfected.

Before we arrive: if water is still spreading

Stop the source.

Close the fixture valve or the interior main shutoff if you can reach it dry. In a multi-unit building, tell the owner or manager to close the building main.

Make the room safe.

Keep power off to wet areas only if the panel is dry and easy to reach. Do not stand in water near outlets.

Lift and move.

Raise electronics, paper and small furniture off wet floors, and pull rugs up where you can.

Record the damage.

Older brick and wood-frame buildings

A state housing plan using 2015 American Community Survey data counted 8,090 housing units in Newburyport, with about half built in 1939 or earlier and roughly 62 percent before 1960. That is an old housing stock by any measure. Old buildings carry cast iron and galvanized drains that corrode, balloon-framed walls that let water drop several floors and stone or brick basements that stay damp.

For a homeowner, the practical point is that water in an older Newburyport house rarely stays where it started. A ceiling stain on the second floor may mean wet insulation in the wall and a damp basement below. Crews use moisture meters and thermal imaging to find the full path, and the repair scope follows from what they find rather than from the visible stain.

Homes built before 1978 may have lead paint, and the EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting rule applies to paid work that disturbs paint. Ask any contractor whether their firm follows it before demolition starts.

Waterfront, marsh and the wastewater plant

The city's wastewater treatment plant sits on the Merrimack River in the coastal floodplain, and a state climate resilience case study describes it as largely below the current FEMA Base Flood Elevation. That fact is a reminder that the whole riverfront shares the same exposure. Basements near the waterfront can take water from storm surge or from stormwater and sewer systems that surcharge when the river is high.

Massachusetts publishes a hurricane evacuation zone map for Newburyport that shows areas flooded by a worst-case surge. It does not tell you whether you are in a FEMA flood zone, so check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer whether you have flood coverage. Standard homeowners policies do not cover flood, according to the Massachusetts Division of Insurance.

Utilities, permits and paperwork

Newburyport's water comes from surface reservoirs including the Artichoke reservoirs, and the Lower Artichoke Reservoir is a drinking-water source for Newburyport, Newbury and West Newbury according to a state document. The city's Building Department says it issues permits and performs inspections for all alterations and repairs, so ask it whether your rebuild needs a permit before work starts.

For an insurance claim, keep photos, a written scope, drying records and receipts. The Division of Insurance says to photograph the exterior and interior, including water height. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, and gradual leaks often are not, so check your policy and ask your insurer.
