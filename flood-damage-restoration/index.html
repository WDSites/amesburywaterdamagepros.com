Emergency Flood Damage Restoration in Amesbury, MA

Storm water, river water and basement flooding carry contamination. Dispatch is open 24/7 for flood damage restoration across Amesbury and nearby towns.

Call (978) 252-8637

Flood damage restoration is the response to storm water, river water, groundwater and sewer overflow entering a building, and in Amesbury that usually means a flooded basement or ground floor. If water is coming in now, call our 24/7 dispatch line at (978) 252-8637. Stay out of the water if electrical outlets, the panel or appliances are in it, call the utility or 911 about any electrical or gas risk, and keep children and pets away from the space.

Flood water is different from a plumbing leak because of where it came from. Water that has crossed yards, roads and storm drains is usually Category 3, meaning it should be treated as contaminated. That changes what can be saved and how the space is cleaned, which is why this page is separate from a standard water mitigation job.

When flood water is an emergency

Treat it as an emergency when water is still rising, when it is above the level of outlets, when it entered from the sewer or a drain, or when you smell fuel or sewage. Do not walk into a flooded basement to move things. Hidden hazards include live electrical equipment, a heating system with a fuel line, and floor drains that may be backing up.

Do not pump a flooded basement out all at once if the ground outside is still saturated. Water pressure outside can crack a wall or lift a slab if the basement empties too fast. A crew will remove water in stages and watch the walls. Never run sewage or flood water into a storm drain, which the city's stormwater ordinance prohibits for sewage lines.

Why Amesbury floods

Amesbury sits on the Powow River, which drains into the Merrimack, with Lake Gardner, the Back River and Clark's Pond all inside or beside the city.

The city's Community Resilience Building matrix lists the flood and drainage concerns residents face. It names the Powow River downtown retaining wall and embankments and erosion on the Merrimack. It also lists undersized culverts on the Back River and Clark's Pond under Route 110 and Elm Street, the Lake Gardner dam and flood storage, and road flooding on Elm Street, Pleasant Valley Road and Merrill Street. Downtown, the Main Street Bridge and the Upper Millyard are listed as concerns.

FEMA's Flood Insurance Study for Essex County, revised in 2025, covers Amesbury and lists flood profiles for the Powow and Merrimack Rivers. The Back River, shared with Salisbury, is mapped as Zone A, which FEMA defines as the 1 percent annual chance floodplain. To see where your address falls, check the FEMA flood map for your address, or ask your insurer.

A flash flood in 2023 showed that flooding is not only a river problem. The city posted that the 4th Street neighborhood is prone to flooding and received drainage improvements, and a federal disaster declaration in 2006 for severe storms and flooding listed Essex County. Both are reminders that storms can overload drainage even where no river overtops.

Basement flooding cleanup

Basements take most Amesbury flood claims. Water enters through the floor slab, through cracks in stone or block walls, through window wells and through sewer or sump failure. The first step is stopping or controlling the source, and the second is pumping in controlled stages.

Once the water is out, wet drywall, insulation, carpet and stored items are removed if they were soaked by contaminated water. Concrete and masonry are scrubbed, treated and dried with dehumidifiers and air movers. Moisture in a basement can linger longer than in an upstairs room, so crews take readings for several days.

Restoration is not waterproofing. If your basement floods repeatedly, a drainage or waterproofing contractor needs to address the cause, and the city has suggested a sump pump for homes that flood regularly.

Sewage backup cleanup

Sewage backup is Category 3 water in every case. It carries bacteria and should be treated as a safety event. Stay out of the space, keep people and pets out, and call the restoration line.

Crews wear protective equipment, contain the affected area, remove porous materials that touched the water, clean and disinfect hard surfaces and use HEPA air filtration to control odor. Drywall and insulation that were wet with sewage are removed rather than dried. If a backup came from a private lateral, a plumber has to clear or repair it before restoration finishes. Ask whether a municipal sewer problem could be involved and report it to your city.

Water categories in flood work

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard describes three water categories, and flood work is mostly about Category 2 and 3. Ask any company you call whether its crews work to S500.

How a flood job runs

  1. Make it safe.Electrical and gas risks are handled first, and the source is controlled.
  2. Extract in stages.Pumps and extraction units remove water at a controlled pace.
  3. Remove and clean.Contaminated porous materials leave, and surfaces are scrubbed and treated.
  4. Dry and monitor.Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach a dry standard.
  5. Rebuild.Repairs begin after drying, described on our water damage repair page.

Flood insurance and your claim

Standard homeowners policies do not cover flood. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says sump pump failure and water backup are covered only with an added endorsement, and that sewer or drain backup and seepage are covered by a flood policy only when an area flood is the cause. Flood insurance through the National Flood Insurance Program usually takes effect after about 30 days, and Massachusetts does not require it by law.

The Division of Insurance says to photograph the exterior and interior of the property, including water height. The city's 2023 notice told residents with flood damage to file an insurance claim immediately and noted that FEMA relief may be unlikely. Check your policy and ask your insurer, and call the Division's Consumer Service Unit at 617-521-7794, option 2, with coverage questions.

Flood damage questions

Do you handle flood damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Amesbury?

Yes. Dispatch is open 24/7, every day, and flooding is treated as a priority. Same-day response is available when crews are available, and we do not promise an arrival time. Stay out of flood water near electrical equipment.

Is flood water always contaminated?

Flood water from rivers, storm drains and sewers should be treated as contaminated, which is Category 3 under S500. Crews remove porous materials it touched and disinfect the space.

Does homeowners insurance cover a flooded basement?

Standard homeowners policies do not cover flood, and sump pump or backup coverage needs an added endorsement. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

How do I find out if my address is in a flood zone?

Check the FEMA flood map for your address, or ask your insurer or the city. FEMA's study for Essex County covers Amesbury.

Will you waterproof my basement?

No. Flood restoration dries and repairs damage that already happened. A drainage or waterproofing contractor addresses the cause.
