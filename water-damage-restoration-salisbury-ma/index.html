Storm season, high tides and Salisbury water

Salisbury's water problems follow the calendar. Winter storms push surge into the beach neighborhoods, and when that surge lands on an astronomically high tide the water goes further than anyone planned for. The town's own flood page names exactly that combination, plus river and stream flooding from the Merrimack and Blackwater Rivers, Town Creek and Smallpox Brook.

The same page says floodwaters can cover many blocks two feet deep or more, and that flood maps are available at Town Hall on Beach Road. For a homeowner that means a first-floor flood is not a freak event in the shore neighborhoods. It is a documented possibility, and it should shape what you ask your insurer and where you keep valuables.

Dispatch takes calls in every season, and crews plan drying differently for a humid summer loss than for a freezing winter one, because cold air holds little moisture and slows evaporation.

When water is spreading in Salisbury

If water is rising or entering a home near the beach, call our 24/7 line right away at (978) 252-8637. Same-day response is available when crews are available, and we do not promise an arrival time. Roads on the shore side can be closed or flooded, so give dispatch your street and tell them which way in is passable.

Salt water and storm surge are Category 3 water under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard because of what they carry from streets, marsh and drains. Porous materials they touch usually have to come out. Keep children and pets away, and stay out if the water is near outlets or the panel.

Summer cottages turned year-round homes

Salisbury's housing plan says the town converted a high number of summer cottages to year-round living units. The plan gives about a third of the housing as built before 1960, among 4,429 total units, so this is not a town of mostly old houses. It is a town of many buildings that were designed for summer use and later lived in all year.

That shapes water damage. Cottage-era construction often has thin walls, minimal insulation and crawl spaces or low slabs close to grade. Pipes run in exposed spaces that freeze when winter conditions arrive. A year-round family in a former cottage often finds out about the weak points during the first hard freeze or the first coastal storm.

After drying, repairs on these homes are usually shorter than on large masonry houses, but the work must account for moisture in framing that never had much airflow. Our water damage repair page explains what rebuild covers.

Floodplain exposure by the numbers

Salisbury's 2026 to 2031 Housing Production Plan counts 1,903 structures in the 100-year and 500-year floodplains, which is 31.4 percent of the town's structures, and says the two zones cover 44 percent of the town's area. The Open Space and Recreation Plan says a 10-year storm could flood about 1,646 buildings, or 30.3 percent, by 2050, and that more than half of the coastal area is salt marsh.

A consultant's coastal resilience study reports that the beach access roads, Beach Road, Old County Road and State Reservation Road, are frequently overtopped, and that undersized, aging culverts restrict marsh drainage. If your home sits behind those roads, drainage and access both matter when you plan.

Standard homeowners policies do not cover flood, according to the Massachusetts Division of Insurance, and flood insurance usually takes effect after about 30 days. If you have not checked the FEMA flood map for your address, do that now. Our flood damage restoration page covers claims and the cleanup of Category 2 and 3 water.

Wells, iron and the building department

Salisbury runs its own Water Division using four groundwater wells. The town's 2024 consumer confidence report lists iron and manganese above secondary standards, which are aesthetic limits that affect taste, color and staining. Reddish staining on fixtures and laundry is a known water-quality note and not a leak, so check for a leak before you assume the plumbing is failing.

The Building Department says permits are required to construct, enlarge, alter, repair, move or demolish, and its FAQ exempts ordinary repairs and cosmetic finishes such as paint and flooring. The word ordinary is not defined, so ask the inspector whether your rebuild qualifies. Permits go through the town's online system.

The Blackwater River runs through the northeast section, and Town Creek runs through the salt marsh near Mudnock and Bridge Roads. Does insurance cover storm surge in Salisbury?

Standard homeowners policies do not cover flood, and surge is usually treated as flood. Check your policy and ask your insurer about flood coverage.

Is rust-colored water a sign of a leak?

Not usually. The town's 2024 report lists iron and manganese above aesthetic standards, which can stain fixtures. A leak shows up as damp materials, not stained tap water.

Do I need a permit to repair flood damage in Salisbury?

It depends. The town exempts ordinary repairs and cosmetic finishes, but does not define ordinary, so ask the Building Department.
