What to do in the first hour

Emergency water damage rewards calm, ordered action. Work through these steps while you wait for the crew, and only do the ones that are safe to reach.

  1. Stop the source.Shut the fixture valve or the main water valve if you can reach it without standing in water. The city defines the curb stop as the valve that shuts water off from the main to your service, but it gives no homeowner instructions, so find your interior main shutoff before an emergency.
  2. Protect the electrical side.If water is near outlets, a panel or a power strip, stay out and call the utility or 911 before you call us. Cut power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and easy to reach.
  3. Lift what you can.Move rugs, small furniture, electronics and paper off wet floors. Pull wet cushions and boxes up onto dry surfaces.
  4. Photograph everything.Take wide and close photos of the damage, the source and any water height on walls before anything is moved. These photos support an insurance claim.
  5. Call the 24/7 line.Tell dispatch what is wet, how long it has been wet and whether the water looks clean, gray or contaminated.

Why Amesbury buildings take on water

Amesbury sits where the Powow River meets the Merrimack, and the city's own Community Resilience Building matrix lists the flood and drainage problems residents live with.

It names the Powow River downtown, embankment erosion on the Merrimack, undersized culverts under Route 110 and Elm Street on the Back River and Clark's Pond, the Lake Gardner dam and flood storage, and road flooding on Elm Street and Pleasant Valley Road. If your home sits near one of those, ask your insurer what your policy covers before the next storm, and check the FEMA flood map for your address.

The housing is old too. A state housing plan citing American Community Survey data put 35 percent of Amesbury's housing units as built before 1940. Old supply lines, stone and rubble foundations, mill-era brick and aging roofs all leak in their own way. In Amesbury Center and the Upper Millyard that means basements and ground floors that stay damp. Around The Point and Deer Island, closeness to the river raises the odds of seepage after heavy rain.

A flash flood in 2023 showed the pattern. The city posted a notice that the 4th Street neighborhood is prone to flooding, told residents with damage to file an insurance claim right away, and suggested a sump pump for homes that flood regularly. The Boston area averages 43.59 inches of precipitation a year and about 91 days with a low at or below freezing, so burst pipes and ice dams matter as much as river water.

Water categories and what they mean for the job

Restoration professionals sort water into three categories, a framework published in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. The category decides what can be dried in place and what must be removed. Ask any company you call whether its crews work to S500.

Time matters because water can move down a category. Clean supply-line water that sits for days in a warm basement can turn into Category 2. Call early and tell dispatch how long the area has been wet.

Insurance and your claim

Working with your insurer

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, is often covered by a homeowners policy. Gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and photograph the damage before anything is moved.

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says sump pump failure and water backup are covered only with an added endorsement, and that flood insurance usually takes about 30 days to take effect.

Equipment you will see on a job

Crews bring moisture meters to find wet material you cannot see, and thermal imaging cameras to spot water behind finished walls. Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces, and LGR dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the air so it does not settle back into drywall and framing. Truck-mounted or portable extraction units and submersible pumps remove standing water first. Antimicrobial treatment and HEPA air filtration come in when the water was contaminated or mold is present.

Water damage and mold

Mold needs moisture, and a wall that stays wet for more than a day or two gives it that. The state's homeowner guide says porous materials that cannot be dried quickly should be discarded, and that no license is required to perform mold remediation in Massachusetts, so ask any company how it contains and cleans to the S520 standard. Our mold remediation page covers containment, removal and what to expect. Health questions about mold belong with your physician.

Do you handle water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Amesbury?

Yes. The dispatch line is open 24/7, every day, and you can call at any hour with active water. Same-day response is available when crews are available, and dispatch will not promise an arrival time it cannot keep. While you wait, stop the source if it is safe and keep people and pets out of any contaminated water.

How fast should water damage be dried?

The Massachusetts homeowner guide on mold says to dry wet areas within 24 hours, and the state Housing Code requires leaks and floods to be dried within 48 hours. Waiting longer raises the chance that drywall, insulation and framing need to be replaced.

Will my homeowners insurance pay for water damage?

It depends on the cause. Sudden and accidental damage such as a burst pipe is often covered, gradual leaks often are not, and flood damage usually needs a separate flood policy. Check your policy and ask your insurer. We document the damage and the scope of work so you have clear records to share.

Is water damage restoration the same as waterproofing?

No. Restoration dries and repairs damage that already happened. Waterproofing changes the building or the grading so water stays out. If your basement keeps taking water, you need a waterproofing or drainage contractor after we dry the space.

Do you need a permit for water damage repairs in Amesbury?

It depends on the work. Amesbury's Inspectional Services files permits online, and electrical, plumbing and gas permits go only to Massachusetts-licensed tradespeople. The city page does not say whether drywall or flooring repair after water damage needs one, so ask Inspectional Services before rebuilding.
