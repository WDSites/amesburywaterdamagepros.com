A frozen pipe is a water line with ice inside it. Ice expands, and the pressure can split the pipe wall or pop a fitting. The real damage usually comes later, when the ice melts and water flows through the break. If water is running now, close the main shut-off and call our 24/7 dispatch line at (978) 252-8637.

Failures that happen with no cold snap involved, such as a corroded joint, are handled on our burst pipe water damage page. This page is about the freeze and the thaw.

Why pipes freeze in New England houses

Boston area climate normals from the National Weather Service count about 91.6 days a year with a low at or below 32 degrees. That is roughly one day in four. A pipe does not freeze the moment the air does, but a stretch of hard cold finds every weak spot in a house.

Roughly a third of Amesbury homes were built before 1940, according to survey data in a state housing plan. Older houses tend to have supply lines run through places that were never meant to hold heat. Lake houses that began as summer cottages are one case, and the Lake Attitash page covers them.

  • Pipes inside exterior walls, especially on the north side.
  • Unheated basements, with lines running near rubble foundation walls or sill plates.
  • Crawl spaces under additions or porches.
  • Garages and breezeways that share a wall with a kitchen or bath.
  • Lines near a hole where cable, dryer vent or a hose bib passes through the wall.

Exposed hose bibs and outdoor spigot lines are another common point of failure when they are not shut off and drained for the season.

Signs a pipe is frozen or already split

  • A faucet that gives only a trickle or nothing, while others work.
  • Frost on a visible pipe, or a pipe that looks swollen.
  • A bulge, a hairline crack or a damp spot on a pipe.
  • Gurgling sounds or a smell from a drain.
  • Stains or dripping appearing after the weather warms up.

The last one is the one people miss. A split pipe may leak nothing while frozen. When the temperature rises or heat comes back on, water follows the break into wall cavities and ceilings.

First-hour safety steps

Shut off the main.

Close it before anything thaws. If a pipe has already split, this stops the flood. The city explains the curb stop as the valve between the water main and your service line, but it gives no steps for finding it, so ask the Water Department.

Open the faucet.

Leave the tap on the frozen line open so melting water has somewhere to go and pressure can drop.

Never use an open flame.

Torches, lighters and propane heaters can ignite framing. Use a hair dryer or warm towels on an accessible pipe only if it is not already cracked.

Stay clear of electricity.

If water reaches outlets or the panel, have the power shut off by the utility or an electrician.

Call a plumber.

A plumber thaws and repairs the line. We do not do plumbing.

What happens when the ice melts

A pipe that split quietly under a floor or behind a wall releases water when it thaws. Because the line is under pressure, the leak can be large. That water often runs for hours before anyone sees it, especially in a vacant house, a seasonal home or a basement nobody visits.

We start where the plumber leaves off. The line is sealed, the water is removed and the structure is dried. For the extraction step, see water mitigation, which explains how drying is measured and finished.

How the drying work goes

  1. Confirm the leak is stopped.A plumber has to repair the split before crews can finish drying.
  2. Find the wet area.Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera trace where the water traveled, including cavities that look dry.
  3. Remove standing water.Submersible pumps and extraction equipment pull it off the floor.
  4. Open and dry.Wet insulation comes out. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run in the affected rooms, with readings taken daily.
  5. Verify.The crew documents dry readings before drywall, flooring or paint go back in.

Insurance after a freeze

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says a burst pipe is usually a homeowners claim when the damage is sudden and accidental. Policies can differ on vacant homes or damage from neglect, so check your policy and ask your insurer about conditions like heating requirements. Flood from outside the house is separate and standard homeowners policies do not cover it.

Take photographs of the split pipe, the wet rooms and the plumber's repair before cleanup begins.

Prevention before the cold

  • Know where your main shut-off is and test that it closes.
  • Seal gaps where lines pass through exterior walls and sills.
  • Keep heat on in a seasonal or vacant home.
  • Open cabinet doors under kitchen and bath sinks on exterior walls during hard cold.
  • Disconnect hoses and shut off outdoor spigots.

Insulating a pipe or rerouting a line is plumbing work. Inspectional Services issues plumbing permits only to Massachusetts-licensed tradespeople.

Frozen pipe questions

Is emergency frozen pipe water damage service available in Amesbury?

Yes. Cold snaps do not follow office hours, and neither does dispatch. Crew timing depends on availability and no arrival time is promised. Shut the main, leave the frozen section alone and keep electrical items away from the water.

Can I thaw a frozen pipe myself?

Only in a limited way. Warm air from a hair dryer on an accessible, uncracked pipe is safer than a flame. Never use a torch. If you see a crack or cannot reach the pipe, call a plumber.

Why did water appear only after the pipe thawed?

Ice can plug a split. While frozen, nothing leaks. When it melts, water flows through the break at full line pressure, and that is when the damage starts.

Will homeowners insurance cover frozen pipe damage?

It often does if the damage is sudden and accidental, but policies vary. Check your policy and ask your insurer about conditions on heat and vacancy.

Does a frozen pipe always burst?

No. Many thaw without splitting. Look for a bulge, crack or damp spot, and ask a plumber to check the line after it thaws.
