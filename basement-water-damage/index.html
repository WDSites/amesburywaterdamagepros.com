How water gets into an Amesbury basement

Most basement events here come from a short list of causes.

  • Seepage through stone or block walls after a long rain or fast snowmelt.
  • Water rising through the floor slab or the joint where the slab meets the wall.
  • A sump pump that failed, lost power or could not keep up with the inflow.
  • Window wells that fill and leak through the frame or glass.
  • A washer hose, water heater or supply line failing inside the basement.
  • A floor drain backing up from the sewer or a private drain line.

The first group is groundwater. The last two are plumbing and drain events, and they carry different risks. River and storm flooding is a bigger scale of the same problem, covered on our flood damage restoration page. Sewage is handled on our sewage backup cleanup page.

Clean water, gray water and black water

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard sorts water into three categories. The category decides what is saved and what is removed.

CategoryTypical basement sourceWhat it means
Category 1Broken supply line, clean seepage right after a rainDried in place if caught fast
Category 2Sump overflow, washer discharge, seepage that satPorous materials removed, space sanitized
Category 3Sewer backup, river or storm flood waterContained, porous materials removed, disinfected

Water that sits for days often moves up a category as bacteria grow. Basement seepage carries soil and often counts as Category 2 or higher. A plumber, not a restoration crew, fixes a failed supply line first.

Safety steps for the first hour

Cut the power.

Do not wade into water that touches outlets, the panel, a furnace or appliances. If the panel is in the wet area, call the utility or 911.

Stay on the stairs.

Standing water hides steps, debris and electrical cords. Never enter a flooded basement to move things.

Shut off the source.

For a plumbing leak, close the shutoff valve. Groundwater cannot be shut off, so call us.

Check heating fuel.

If you smell fuel or gas, leave and call 911 or the gas utility.

Photograph before you move.

Photograph the water height and your belongings from the stairs for the claim.

Finished and unfinished basements

An unfinished basement with a concrete floor and bare walls usually dries well. Hard surfaces get cleaned and treated, and stored items are the main loss. A finished basement is harder because water goes behind the finish.

  • Carpet and pad are removed in most cases, especially after Category 2 water.
  • Drywall that wicked water is cut off above the wet line, and insulation behind it is removed.
  • Wood paneling, trim and cabinets are checked with moisture meters and often dried in place.
  • Subfloors and framing are opened if readings stay high.

Thermal imaging helps find wet wall cavities without cutting into every wall.

Stored belongings

Basements hold boxes, tools, holiday items and furniture. Cardboard and fabric absorb water and mold quickly. Photograph items before they move, set aside what matters most, and bring wet items out only after the area is safe. Documents and photos can sometimes be dried by a specialist. Ask us before you throw away anything an insurer may need to see.

How the pump-out and drying work

  1. Pump in stages.Submersible pumps remove water at a controlled pace so saturated soil outside does not push a wall inward.
  2. Extract and remove.A truck-mounted unit or portable extractors take the remaining water, and soaked materials are removed.
  3. Open and measure.Moisture meters and thermal imaging show wet walls, slab and framing.
  4. Dry the structure.Air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and desiccant units run until readings reach a dry standard. Masonry and slabs dry slowly.
  5. Clean, treat and monitor.Surfaces are cleaned, treated with antimicrobial where needed, and checked on return visits.

Basement dehumidification often needs more days than an upstairs room. For the wider picture, see water mitigation.

What we do not fix

We do not waterproof, install or repair sump pumps, fix foundation cracks or regrade yards. A drainage or waterproofing contractor handles those. Repairs involving plumbing or electrical work need licensed tradespeople, and the city's Inspectional Services handles permits. Ask them whether drywall or flooring repair needs one, because the city page does not say.

Insurance and sump pumps

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says standard homeowners policies do not cover flood. Sump pump failure or water backup is covered only with an added endorsement, so check whether yours has one. Sewer or drain backup and seepage are covered by a flood policy only when an area flood is the cause.

FEMA's Flood Insurance Study for Essex County, revised in 2025, maps the Back River as Zone A, so check the FEMA flood map for your address. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

Prevention and mistakes to avoid

Test a sump pump before storm season and consider a battery backup if power often fails. Keep gutters and downspouts clear and aimed away from the foundation. Do not store paper or cloth directly on the floor. Do not run a household fan to push damp air around, and do not seal wet walls with paint.

The city's resilience plan lists road flooding on Route 110 and Elm Street, so heavy rain is a good reminder to check your sump. Neighbors in Salisbury and Merrimac can reach us the same way.

Basement questions

Is emergency basement water damage service available in Amesbury?

Yes. Dispatch is answered around the clock, and basement calls with rising water are treated as urgent. No arrival time is promised. Stay out of standing water near electrical equipment and call the utility if the panel is wet.

Why does my stone foundation leak after rain?

Stone and mortar are porous, and wet soil presses water through joints. Restoration dries the damage. A waterproofing or drainage contractor addresses the entry points so it does not repeat.

Is basement water always contaminated?

Not always. Clean supply-line water is Category 1, but seepage that sat becomes Category 2, and sewer or flood water is Category 3. The category decides what is removed.

Does insurance cover a sump pump failure?

Only with an added sump pump or water backup endorsement, per the Massachusetts Division of Insurance. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

How long does a wet basement take to dry?

Often several days, and longer for stone, block and slab. Crews take readings until materials reach a dry standard rather than guessing by the calendar.
