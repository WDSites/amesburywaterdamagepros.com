Lake Attitash and its cottages

Lake Attitash sits on the line between Amesbury and Merrimac in the northwest of Amesbury. A state landscape inventory report for Essex County describes the Amesbury side as an early 20th century recreation area with narrow roads and summer cottages, many now year-round houses. The Massachusetts Historical Commission's survey of Amesbury calls Lake Attitash, which it also names Kimball's Pond, the largest body of water in the town.

Roads and homes line much of the shore, and the lake is shared. The Merrimac shore has its own page, water damage restoration in Merrimac, and this page covers the Amesbury side. For the whole city, see the Amesbury homepage.

The lake is not the flood story here

The city's resilience matrix lists Lake Attitash, but for water quality, naming invasive species, runoff, fertilizer and algal blooms. That is not a flood record, and we do not treat it as one. We found no source naming flood damage to homes at the lake. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified, so check the FEMA flood map for your address.

Water damage here is more likely to start inside the house. Cottages built for summer often have plumbing and insulation that were never meant for winter. After a house is converted, owners add heat, baths and laundry, and the old lines and floors take on more use than they were built for. That is a general pattern, not a count of local homes.

Winter, vacancy and long leaks

A seasonal or part-time house can lose water for days or weeks before anyone sees it. A line splits in a cold spell, or a hose fails while the owner is away, and the first sign is a soft floor or a musty smell. The longer water runs, the more the structure holds. Tell dispatch how long the house was empty.

Pipes that freeze usually split, and the real flooding starts at the thaw. Read frozen pipe water damage if you are dealing with that. For a pipe that has failed without a freeze, see burst pipe water damage.

Foundations, crawl spaces and basements

Lake houses vary in how they sit on the ground. Some have full basements, some have crawl spaces and some sit on slabs. The sources we found do not say which is common at Attitash, so the crew learns on arrival. If water is under the house, crawl space water damage and basement water damage explain what changes.

Wet crawl space insulation and subfloors can stay damp for a long time because the air under a house moves slowly. Drying them means opening the area, removing wet insulation and running dehumidifiers. The ANSI/IICRC S500 reference standard describes how drying goals are set. Do not enter a crawl space with standing water or live wiring.

First hour safety steps

  • Shut off the main water valve if you can reach it safely.
  • Stay out of standing water near wiring, outlets or a panel.
  • Do not enter a flooded crawl space.
  • Photograph the damage and the failed part before it is thrown out.
  • Move belongings from wet floors where it is safe.
  • Call a plumber about the pipe, because we dry and clean but do not repair plumbing.

Insurance and documentation

A sudden pipe failure is commonly treated as a homeowners claim. Damage from a leak that ran slowly or went unnoticed may be treated differently. Seasonal or part-time use can raise questions about how a policy applies, so ask your insurer directly. Keep photos, the plumber's findings and moisture readings. They describe the damage but do not guarantee coverage.

Lake Attitash water damage questions

Do Lake Attitash homes flood from the lake?

We found no source naming lake flooding of homes. The city lists the lake for water quality, not flooding. Check the FEMA map for your address and ask your insurer about flood coverage.

My cottage froze while I was away. What now?

Shut off the main if you can do it safely, photograph what you see and call dispatch. The pipe itself needs a plumber, and we handle drying and cleanup once the water is stopped.

Can you dry a crawl space under a lake house?

Often yes, if the space is safe to work in. The crew removes wet insulation and runs dehumidifiers. We do not do waterproofing or drainage repairs.

Does a mold smell mean mold is growing?

It can. Damp materials and a musty smell after a leak should be checked quickly. See mold remediation for how it is handled.
