Amesbury Water Damage Pros is a dispatch line for water damage and mold problems in Amesbury, Massachusetts and the towns around it. The address is 77 Elm Street, Amesbury, MA 01913, and the phone is (978) 252-8637. The line is open 24/7, every day of the year.

What we do

When you call, dispatch asks what is wet, where the water came from and how long it has been wet. From those answers we route the job to licensed and insured restoration professionals who handle extraction, drying, cleanup, repair and mold work. Scope and price are explained before work starts.

The services are emergency water removal, water mitigation, flood damage restoration, water damage cleanup, water damage repair and mold remediation.

What we do not do

We do not offer waterproofing, foundation repair or plumbing repair. If your basement takes water repeatedly, a drainage or waterproofing contractor addresses the cause. If a pipe has burst, a licensed plumber repairs it. We also do not promise arrival times or insurance outcomes. Same-day response is available when crews are available.

How we write about local conditions

Pages on this site use public sources for local facts, such as the City of Amesbury, the Federal Emergency Management Agency, the National Weather Service, the Massachusetts Division of Insurance and the state's homeowner guidance on mold. Where a source does not give an answer, the page says to ask the city or check your policy. If you find an error, tell us by phone and we will correct the page.

Why a dispatch line instead of a single crew

Water damage rarely arrives at a convenient hour. Pipes fail at night, storms hit on weekends and a ceiling can come down on a holiday.

A single small crew cannot cover every hour, so the line routes each call to licensed and insured restoration professionals who can take the job, and that is how same-day response stays possible when crews are available.

We say when crews are available because we will not promise an arrival time that depends on traffic, weather and other emergencies.

For homeowners this means one number to call whether the problem is a flooded basement in Amesbury or a leak in a Haverhill triple-decker. You describe the damage once, dispatch records the details and the crew arrives with a plan for the first steps. Scope and price are explained before any work starts, so there are no surprises on the first day.

What a good restoration company does

Whoever you call, a few things separate careful work from rushed work. The crew should take moisture readings rather than guess, explain which materials will be removed and why, document the loss with photos and notes your insurer can use, and leave drying equipment running until readings reach a dry standard. Ask for a written scope. Ask what happens if the job grows once walls are open.

You should also expect plain answers about what restoration cannot do. It does not waterproof a basement, it does not repair a failed foundation and it does not replace a plumber or a septic contractor. A company that promises to fix the cause of every leak is overpromising.

How we handle insurance questions

We do not file claims for you, and we do not promise any outcome.

What we can do is document the damage, take photographs and moisture readings, and explain the scope of work in writing so you have records to share. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance explains that standard homeowners policies do not cover flood, that sump pump failure and water backup need an added endorsement and that sudden and accidental damage such as a burst pipe is usually a homeowners claim.

Read your own policy, and ask your insurer when you are unsure.

Industry standards

Restoration work follows published standards, including ANSI/IICRC S500 for water damage restoration and S520 for mold remediation. We name them because homeowners deserve a way to compare companies. Ask any company you call whether its crews work to them.
