Hardwood Floor Repair After Water Damage in Amesbury, MA

Wet hardwood can often be saved if it dries slowly and evenly, and the repair you choose depends on how the boards moved.

By the Amesbury Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-10-29

Do not sand a wet floor. That is the mistake that ruins the most hardwood after a leak. Boards swell when they absorb water and shrink as they dry, and the repair only makes sense once the wood and the subfloor under it have settled at a normal moisture level.

What water does to a wood floor

Solid and engineered hardwood is hygroscopic, which means it takes on and gives up moisture with the air around it. When a floor gets soaked from above or from below, the boards swell. The problem is that they swell unevenly, and the shape they take tells you where the water came from.

Cupping is the most common result. The edges of each board rise higher than the center, which usually means moisture came from below, such as a slab, crawlspace or leaking pipe. Crowning is the reverse, with a raised center, and it often shows up after a cupped floor was sanded too early. Buckling is the worst case, where boards lift off the subfloor in a ridge or tent.

Cupping, crowning and buckling compared

Each symptom points to a different repair path, so match what you see before you decide.

Sheet vinyl and laminate often look similar, but they react differently. If you are not sure what you have, a crew can pull a floor register and look at the edge of a board.

Slow, controlled drying comes first

Hardwood should not be dried fast. Pushing hot, dry air across a wet floor can split boards and set cups permanently. Crews use air movers to circulate air, LGR dehumidifiers to pull moisture from the room, and sometimes a mat system that draws moisture out through the floor surface.

A moisture meter takes readings on the boards, the subfloor and a dry reference board elsewhere in the house. The floor is dry when those numbers match. That can take a week or more for dense oak, and rushing it is how flat floors turn into rolling ones. When drying works, many cupped floors flatten by themselves and need only a light sanding.

Check the subfloor before you pick a repair

A pretty floor over a wet subfloor is a bad bet. Plywood and board subflooring hold water longer than the finish does, and they can grow mold if trapped under new boards. Before any sanding or installation, crews check the subfloor with a meter and, where needed, lift a few boards to look.

Moisture trapped below can also telegraph through new boards months later. If the subfloor is damaged, the section is cut out and replaced. That work is part of water damage repair, and it comes after the structure has reached a dry standard in water mitigation.

Sand and refinish, board replacement or full replacement

Most homeowners face three paths.

  1. Sand and refinish.Best for light cupping or crowning after the floor has dried flat. A drum or orbital sander removes the top layer, then stain and finish go on in coats.
  2. Replace damaged boards.Best when only part of the room is buckled or stained. New boards are interwoven with the old, then the whole floor is sanded so the color blends.
  3. Replace the full floor.Best when most boards are warped, rotted or loose, or when the subfloor needs major work. It costs the most but resolves the issue.

A thin engineered floor can usually be sanded only once or twice, because the wear layer is shallow. Solid hardwood can handle several refinishes. This is one reason to ask what you have before choosing.

Insurance basics

A sudden and accidental event, such as a burst pipe or a failed dishwasher line, is often covered by a homeowners policy. Gradual seepage often is not, and flood from outside needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer, and photograph the floor before any boards come up.

Hardwood floor questions

Will cupped hardwood go flat on its own?

Often, if the water source is stopped and the floor dries slowly. Wait for stable moisture readings before deciding on sanding. Sanding too early can leave the floor crowned.

How long should I wait before sanding?

Until the boards and subfloor read dry against a reference board, which can take weeks for thick or dense wood. A meter is the only reliable check.

Can buckled hardwood be saved?

Sometimes if only a few boards lifted and they can be dried and re-fastened. Heavy buckling usually means replacing those boards and checking the subfloor.

Is engineered flooring harder to fix than solid hardwood?

Often, because the wear layer is thin and glued flooring can delaminate when wet. Solid boards can usually be sanded more times.
