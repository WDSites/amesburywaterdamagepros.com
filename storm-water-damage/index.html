Where storm water gets in

The National Weather Service Boston normals for 1991 to 2020 show annual precipitation of 43.59 inches in the Boston area. Water also arrives at an angle in coastal storms, which tests parts of a house that stay dry in ordinary rain.

  • Roof leaks at worn shingles, flashing, chimneys, skylights and vent pipes.
  • Wind driven rain through window and door frames, and behind loose siding.
  • Limbs or whole trees that puncture a roof, which also opens the attic and ceilings.
  • Gutters and downspouts that overflow and send water down the wall or into the foundation.
  • Ice dams and clogged drains, when winter weather leaves water nowhere to go.
  • Basement entry from saturated ground, which leads into our basement water damage work.

In older houses, water can run along rafters and sheathing for several feet before it shows on a ceiling. The stain is rarely under the actual hole.

Signs and what they usually mean

A brown ring on a ceiling suggests a roof or attic leak. A bubbled paint patch near a window points to water around the frame. Wet insulation that clumps or sags in the attic means the roof has been open for a while. Water dripping from a light fixture is an electrical hazard, so turn off the circuit if you can do it safely and stay clear.

A musty smell in an upstairs room after a storm is a warning even when you see no stain. Water inside a wall cavity stays hidden until the wall is opened or scanned.

Safety steps for the first hour

Watch for electrical hazards.

Do not touch fixtures, outlets or cords near wet ceilings. If a limb hit a power line, stay away and call 911 and the utility.

Stay out of damaged rooms.

A wet ceiling can collapse. Move people and pets out and do not stand under a bulging patch.

Contain what you can.

Use buckets and towels under drips, and move furniture away.

Call a roofer for the roof.

A roofer tarps and repairs the roof. We do not do roofing, tree removal or electrical work.

Photograph everything.

Take photos of the damage, the fallen tree and the roof from the ground before cleanup.

Power loss and sump failure

Big storms often take out power. A sump pump with no electricity stops pumping just as the ground fills with water, and a basement can flood in hours. If your home has a battery backup, check that it is charged. If it does not, avoid the basement and call us if water reaches the floor. Never use a generator indoors or in an attached garage, because carbon monoxide can kill.

How we dry a storm loss

  1. Stabilize the interior.We assess the damage after the roof is tarped or the opening is closed, or work around it when that is not yet possible.
  2. Extract standing water.Wet carpet, pad and floors are extracted with portable or truck-mounted units.
  3. Remove soaked materials.Wet ceiling drywall, insulation and flooring that cannot dry are cut out and bagged.
  4. Dry and dehumidify.Air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and moisture meters track drying in ceilings, attics and wall cavities. Thermal imaging finds hidden damp areas.
  5. Clean and prepare for repair.Surfaces are cleaned and treated, and rebuild work is described on our water damage repair page.

Drying follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard as a published reference. Rain water that fell through a roof is often treated differently from water that touched the ground, so the category matters.

Storm cleanup versus river flooding

The two are easy to confuse. Storm water comes from above or the side and is usually treated as lower risk until it touches debris or sits. Flood water rises from rivers, streams or runoff and is treated as contaminated. If water is climbing up your walls from outside, read our flood damage restoration page.

Storm damage questions

Is emergency storm water damage service available in Amesbury?

Yes, including during storms. Roads and crew availability affect timing, and no arrival time is promised. Stay away from dripping light fixtures and wet ceilings, and call a roofer for tarping.

Do you repair the roof?

No. A roofer tarps and repairs the roof. We extract water, remove wet materials and dry the interior, and we can coordinate with your roofer.

Is storm water the same as flood water?

No. Rain through a roof is usually less contaminated than flood water, which rises from rivers, drains and runoff. The difference changes what is removed.

What if a tree fell on my house?

Stay away from power lines, call 911 if anyone is hurt, and call a tree and roof contractor to secure the opening. We dry the interior once water is controlled.

Will insurance cover storm water damage?

Often rain through a damaged roof is a homeowners claim, but flood is not. Check your policy and ask your insurer.
