A town that was once part of Amesbury

Merrimac was Amesbury's West Parish until 1876, when it became its own town, and the two still share Lake Attitash and Route 110. That history matters for water damage because houses in both towns follow the same pattern: small lots, lakeside lanes and mill-era streets, with brooks and ponds running between them. Merrimac sits on the north bank of the Merrimack River, which forms its southern border.

Many of the small houses around Lake Attitash began as seasonal cottages. A state survey notes that early subdivisions on the lake's west shore were small houses later converted to year-round homes. A house built for summer use often has thin walls, little insulation and a low crawl space, and when it becomes a full-time home those details show up as frozen pipes and damp floors.

The housing count in Merrimac's hazard mitigation plan is 2,582 units, and the plan does not give an age breakdown, so this page does not guess one. What the state survey does record is a downtown with Federal-period houses and buildings from the late 1800s around Merrimac Square. Old framing and new plumbing are common neighbors there.

When water is spreading in Merrimac

If water is spreading in a Merrimac home, call our 24/7 dispatch line at (978) 252-8637 at any hour. Same-day response is available when crews are available, and we do not promise an arrival time. Tell dispatch whether the water is clean, from a supply line, or dirty, from a drain or lake edge.

For lakeside homes, water that rises from the ground or through a slab should be treated as Category 2 or 3 water under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. Porous materials it touched, such as carpet pad and insulation, usually have to be removed. Stay out of the space if water is near outlets, a panel or a well pump control.

Before we arrive: if water is still spreading

Check the pump.

If you are on a private well, shut off power to the well pump only if the panel is dry. A pump running into a broken line keeps adding water.

Close the valve.

Shut off the fixture valve or the interior main. Open a low faucet to drain the line once it is off.

Clear the floor.

Lift rugs, boxes and small furniture onto dry surfaces before the water wicks into them.

Document and call.

Photograph the damage and the source, then call dispatch and say how long the area has been wet.

Brooks, lakes and the river

Merrimac's hazard mitigation plan says about 11 percent of town land lies in the 100-year or 500-year flood zones, and that Zone A covers about 513.6 acres. It names Cobbler's Brook, which runs through the town center to the Merrimack, and Lake Attitash, which the town shares with Amesbury, and it says lake-area emergency planning may need a regional solution.

The plan records floods in 1936, 1938, 2006, 2007 and 2010, and calls the 1936 flood the worst on record. The 2006 flood took out part of River Road, and part of that road remains closed after a riverbank failure. If you live along River Road or near the brook, ask your insurer about flood coverage, because standard homeowners policies do not cover flood, according to the Massachusetts Division of Insurance.

The only critical facility the plan lists in a flood zone is the Middle Road sewer lift station on River Road, which has a backup generator. That is useful context if sewage backs up in a home near the river, since a municipal problem would need to be reported to the town.

Wells, town water and the building department

Merrimac Water Department draws groundwater from the Wallace Way and Bear Hill wellfields and treats it for iron and manganese. The hazard plan says about 80 percent of residents are on municipal water, which leaves roughly a fifth who are not. If you are one of them, a failed pump, pressure tank or well line is a separate cause of water in the house, and a licensed well or plumbing contractor handles it.

Inspectional Services, at 4 School Street, takes permit applications online only. The town page does not say whether repair or renovation after water damage needs a permit, so ask Inspectional Services before you rebuild.
