Water Damage Restoration in Point Shore, Amesbury, MA

Point Shore sits where the Powow River meets the Merrimack, so water here can come from the river edge or from the plumbing of a very old house.

Where Point Shore sits and why water finds it

Point Shore is the Amesbury neighborhood on the Merrimack River at the point where the Powow River reaches the larger river. A state landscape inventory report for Essex County, posted on the city website, places it along the Merrimack south of Macy Street, which is Route 110, with Main Street as its main corridor. The same report describes mostly 18th and 19th century homes on the north side of Main Street and boatyards and fishing docks on the river side.

The Massachusetts Historical Commission's survey of Amesbury calls the same area Salisbury Point and notes the late 18th century homes of boat builders. The landscape report also describes a Ferry District beside it along the river. This page covers both, because their water problems share the same river edge. The Amesbury homepage covers the rest of the city.

What the record says about river water here

A photo caption in a Newburyport News gallery says the Merrimack overflowed some on Main Street on Amesbury's Point Shore, and it notes that the 1938 flood mark sits halfway up a flagpole. The gallery is undated, so this page gives no year for that overflow. The Amesbury library's local history collection also holds a 1936 account of the Merrimack flood written by a Point Shore resident.

That is the extent of what we could verify. FEMA's Flood Insurance Study for Essex County lists flood profiles for the Merrimack and Powwow Rivers, but neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified for Point Shore. Check the FEMA flood map for your own address. The city's resilience matrix also lists erosion of the Merrimack embankment as a concern.

What river overflow means for the cleanup

River water is not clean water. Under the ANSI/IICRC S500 reference standard it is treated as Category 3, which means it may carry sewage, soil and chemicals. That changes the job. Carpet, pad, insulation and other porous materials that river water has soaked are usually removed instead of dried. Hard surfaces get cleaned and treated.

The sequence is the same as other flood jobs. Pumps or a truck-mounted extractor remove standing water. Moisture meters map what is still wet, and air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure. Do not try to dry contaminated water with household fans or a shop vacuum. Our flood damage restoration page explains the process in full, and emergency water removal covers the pumping stage.

Older houses and leaks that start indoors

Not every Point Shore call involves the river. A house that has stood for a century or two has had its plumbing, heating and kitchens changed many times, and a failed supply line, water heater or appliance hose makes the same mess as anywhere else. The state reports do not say which Point Shore homes have basements, so the crew finds out on arrival whether the water is in a basement, a crawl space or on a main floor.

If the water came from a pipe, stopping the source and drying quickly matters most. See burst pipe water damage for that case and basement water damage if the water is below grade. General water damage cleanup follows once the structure is dry.

First hour safety steps

  • Stay out of standing water if any outlet, cord or appliance may be live in it.
  • Do not flip breakers while standing in water. Call an electrician or the utility instead.
  • Shut off the main water valve only if you can reach it safely and dry.
  • Keep children and pets out of river water and out of rooms where it has been.
  • Photograph the damage and the water line before moving anything.
  • Lift unaffected belongings off the floor only where it is safe to do so.

Insurance and documentation

Insurers treat river flooding and plumbing failures differently, and the Massachusetts Division of Insurance says a standard homeowners policy does not cover flood. A sudden pipe failure is a different kind of claim. Read your policy, ask your insurer which cause applies to you, and keep photos, moisture readings and any written scope. Documentation helps describe the damage but does not guarantee coverage.

Point Shore water damage questions

Does Point Shore flood?

The sources we found show the Merrimack has reached Main Street here on at least one high water day, and a 1936 flood account exists. We did not verify a FEMA zone for the neighborhood, so check the FEMA map for your address.

What should I do if river water gets into my basement?

Stay out if electrical equipment is wet or the water is rising. Call your utility or an electrician about power, photograph the water line, then call dispatch. River water needs professional extraction and cleaning.

Can a very old house be dried without harm?

Often yes, but results depend on the materials. Old plaster, wide floorboards and framing respond differently, and we cannot promise every material can be saved. The crew checks moisture before deciding what to keep.

Do repairs after water damage need a permit?

The city's Inspectional Services decides, and the city's page does not say whether drywall or flooring repair after water damage needs one. Ask them before rebuilding.
