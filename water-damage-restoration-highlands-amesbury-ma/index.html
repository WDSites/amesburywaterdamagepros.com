Water Damage Cleanup in the Highlands, Amesbury, MA

The Highlands around Hillside Avenue is known for large turn-of-the-century houses, where a leak on an upper floor can soak several rooms below it.

The Highlands and its houses

The Highlands is a residential part of Amesbury that a state historic survey places around Hillside Avenue, Greenleaf Street and Estes Street. The Massachusetts Historical Commission describes it as a later population center, developed between 1870 and 1911, with Shingle, Queen Anne and Classical Revival houses on Hillside Avenue and Queen Anne houses on Greenleaf Street. It says those neighborhoods remain largely intact.

A state landscape inventory report for Essex County, posted on the city website, describes a Highland Street area as a residential neighborhood on a scenic road, known for large Queen Anne and Colonial Revival houses. Those are the houses this page is about. For the city as a whole, see the Amesbury homepage.

Where the water comes from

The sources we checked do not name any flood or drainage concern in the Highlands. The city's resilience matrix does not mention it, and neither survey describes flooding there. We are not going to invent a flood history to make the page sound urgent. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified, so check the FEMA flood map for your address.

Water damage in a big older house usually comes from inside. Supply lines, drain lines, heating pipes, water heaters, roofs and window flashing all age. When one fails, the water drops through floors, which is why the damage is rarely limited to the room where it began.

How a leak travels through a tall house

A bathroom on the second floor sits above a ceiling, a wall and sometimes a kitchen. Water from a failed fitting follows the pipe chase, runs along joists and soaks insulation before a stain shows. By the time a ceiling bulges, the cavity above it may have been wet for a while. The crew uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to follow the path instead of guessing.

Ask the crew about plaster too. Homes of this period often have plaster over wood lath instead of drywall, though that varies house to house. Wet plaster can loosen from the lath, so the crew checks it before deciding what can dry in place. Our page on wall and drywall water damage covers how walls are opened, dried and rebuilt.

Services that match these houses

Common causes in a house like this are a split line, a frozen pipe or a failed tank. Split and frozen lines are covered under burst pipe water damage and frozen pipe water damage. A failed tank is covered under water heater leak cleanup. Once the structure is dry, water damage repair covers the rebuild.

A plumber, roofer or electrician fixes the cause. We dry and clean what the water reached, then help plan the rebuild. If a roof or window leak is the source, the opening has to be sealed by that trade before drying can hold.

First hour safety steps

  • Shut off the main water valve if you can reach it safely.
  • Turn off power to a wet area only from a dry spot, or call an electrician.
  • Do not stand under a bulging or sagging ceiling. Water can bring it down.
  • Photograph the leak, the stain and anything damaged.
  • Place buckets and move furniture away from drips if it is safe.
  • Keep fans and electrical equipment out of standing water.

Drying a multi-floor house

The crew removes standing water, opens wet cavities where the readings call for it and sets air movers and dehumidifiers. Wet insulation is removed because it holds water against framing. The ANSI/IICRC S500 reference standard describes setting drying goals. Old wood floors and trim often take longer to dry than new materials, and we cannot promise every finish will survive.

Insurance and documentation

If a pipe bursts suddenly, that is usually handled as a homeowners claim. Slow leaks that go unnoticed are often treated differently, so ask your insurer how it classifies your case. Photograph the damage, keep any plumber's report and record moisture readings. These records describe the damage but do not guarantee payment.

Highlands water damage questions

Does the Highlands flood?

We found no flood or drainage concern named for the Highlands in the city and state sources we checked. Most water damage there is more likely to start inside a house. Check the FEMA map for your address.

Can you save old plaster walls?

Sometimes. Plaster that is still firmly attached and dries well can often stay. Plaster that sags or crumbles usually has to come out. The crew checks it with moisture readings before deciding.

The stain is on the ceiling. Is the leak above it?

Usually, but not always. Water can travel along joists before it shows. Tell dispatch what is above the stain so the crew can trace the path with a meter.

Will my insurance cover a leak?

That depends on the cause and your policy. Sudden pipe failures are handled differently from gradual leaks. Ask your insurer. We document damage but do not decide coverage.
